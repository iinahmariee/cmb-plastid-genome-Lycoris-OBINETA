# Part E. Questions to Answer

**1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?**

- Scientific name: *Lycoris radiata*
- Plastid genome accession number: MW554924

**2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?**

- Total length: 158,335 bp

**3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.**

- LSC (Large Single‑Copy region): Largest arc on the map; spans across the upper portion between IRb and IRa.
- SSC (Small Single‑Copy region): Shortest arc; located at the bottom of the map between IRa and IRb.
- IRa (Inverted Repeat A): Right‑side arc between LSC and SSC.
- IRb (Inverted Repeat B): Left‑side arc between SSC and LSC.

**4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.**

- *psbA* (Photosystem II protein D1)
- *rbcL* (Ribulose‑1,5‑bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase large subunit)
- *atpA* (ATP synthase subunit alpha)

**5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.**

- *ndhD* (NADH dehydrogenase subunit D)

**6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?**

- *rrn16S* (16S ribosomal RNA gene)
- Yes, the gene appears more than once, specifically in both IRa and IRb, because the inverted repeat regions are identical and duplicated sequences.

**7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.**

- Gene name: *psaA* (Photosystem I P700 chlorophyll a apoprotein A1)
- General biological function: Encodes a core protein subunit of Photosystem I, which functions in the light‑dependent reactions of photosynthesis. It forms part of the reaction center that captures light energy and mediates electron transfer to produce the energy carriers ATP and NADPH.

**8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?**

- The direction of gene arrows and orientation shows that transcription proceeds in both directions around the genome and uses both DNA strands. Genes positioned on the outer rim of the circle are transcribed clockwise; genes positioned on the inner rim are transcribed counter‑clockwise.

**9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.**

- GC content is not uniform across the entire plastid genome where it varies among regions. The inverted repeat regions show higher GC content; the large single‑copy region has moderate GC content; and the small single‑copy region has the lowest GC content.

**10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?**

- A graphical plastid genome map makes structural organization visually clear at a glance: you can immediately identify region boundaries, gene positions, distribution patterns, and transcription directions. Raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations contains the same information but does not show spatial arrangement or overall structure, making it far more difficult to interpret, compare, or understand the overall genome organization.
